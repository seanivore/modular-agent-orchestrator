# 3.1 Behavior: MAO's Operational DNA

## Consistent Personality, Adaptive Intelligence

MAO isn't just software that follows instructions—it has **behavioral patterns** that make it feel like working with a knowledgeable, reliable partner.

These patterns aren't hardcoded quirks or artificial personality traits. They're **operational protocols** designed to maximize effectiveness while maintaining predictable, trustworthy behavior.

## Core Behavioral Protocols

*[Reference to orchestrator/protocol.md]*

MAO's behavior is governed by a sophisticated protocol system that defines how it approaches different types of interactions and decisions.

### Communication Patterns

**Collaborative, Not Subservient**
```
Instead of: "I will do exactly what you tell me to do."
MAO says: "I understand your goal. Based on my analysis, I think we should consider these three approaches. What's your preference?"
```

**Proactive, Not Reactive**
```
Instead of: "Task completed. What's next?"
MAO says: "I've finished the research phase and found some unexpected opportunities. Should I explore these further, or would you prefer to review the current findings first?"
```

**Transparent, Not Mysterious**
```
Instead of: "Processing your request..."
MAO says: "I'm analyzing your requirements and considering three potential workflows. This will take about 30 seconds while I evaluate the best approach."
```

### Decision-Making Patterns

MAO follows consistent logic when making autonomous decisions:

#### 1. **Assess Context**
- What is the user trying to accomplish?
- What constraints and preferences have they expressed?
- What resources and capabilities are available?

#### 2. **Generate Options**
- What are the possible approaches?
- What are the trade-offs of each option?
- Which approaches align best with stated goals?

#### 3. **Apply Judgment**
- Which option is most likely to succeed?
- What are the risks and mitigation strategies?
- When should I involve the human vs. proceed autonomously?

#### 4. **Execute with Monitoring**
- Implement the chosen approach
- Monitor progress and results
- Adjust course if needed
- Report back with insights

### Error Handling Philosophy

When things go wrong, MAO follows predictable patterns:

**Graceful Degradation**
```
"The primary research tool is unavailable, so I'm switching to the backup search method. This might take slightly longer but will still get you comprehensive results."
```

**Transparent Problem-Solving**
```
"I'm encountering an API rate limit. I can either wait 60 seconds for it to reset, switch to a different provider, or break this into smaller requests. What's your preference?"
```

**Learning from Failures**
```
"The initial approach didn't work as expected. I've identified the issue and have two alternative strategies. I'll also update my approach for similar situations in the future."
```

## Conversation Flow Patterns

*[Reference to orchestrator/protocol.md conversation flow sections]*

MAO's conversations follow natural, productive patterns:

### Initial Engagement
1. **Acknowledge the goal** clearly and specifically
2. **Ask clarifying questions** to understand context
3. **Propose an approach** with reasoning
4. **Confirm understanding** before proceeding

### During Execution
1. **Provide progress updates** at logical milestones
2. **Flag important decisions** that might affect outcomes
3. **Share interesting discoveries** that could influence direction
4. **Request input** when human judgment is valuable

### Completion and Handoff
1. **Summarize what was accomplished** with key insights
2. **Highlight important findings** or unexpected results
3. **Suggest next steps** based on outcomes
4. **Document the process** for future reference
